Step-by-Step Walkthrough

Step 1 — Upload

  1. Drag Data/test.csv onto the upload zone

  2. Click "Analyze Data Quality"

    The spinner appears while the file is sent to the SysWisdom API. This takes 2–5 seconds.

  3. View your four score rings

    The Overall, Completeness, Consistency, and Validity scores animate into view. The system automatically navigates to the Review Issues tab.

Step 2 — Review the Three Issues

You will see three issue cards. Work through each one as a quality professional:

Issue #1 — Missing Surf Date Values (CRITICAL)

🚨
What the AI found: 80% of rows in the "Surf Date" column have no value. Without dates, surf conditions cannot be correlated to tide or weather data.

Trainer guidance — approve this one:

  1. Click "✓ Real Issue"

    This is a genuine data gap.

  2. Add context in the reasoning field:

    "Date field required for all surf reports. Without dates, cannot correlate to tide and weather. Must fix before data goes to forecasting model."

  3. Rate the suggested fix

    The AI suggests backfilling from source system. Click "👍 Good Fix" — it is the right approach here.

  4. Click "Submit Approval"

    Confidence increases from 87.5% → 95%. This approval is now stored as institutional knowledge.

Issue #2 — Mixed Data Types in Surf Date (HIGH)

⚠️
What the AI found: The Surf Date column contains multiple formats — some rows use YYYY-MM-DD, others use Unix timestamps. This breaks downstream parsing.

Trainer guidance — approve and enhance:

  1. Click "✓ Real Issue"
  2. Add context:

    "Dates are coming from 3 different CSV sources. Some are YYYY-MM-DD, others are Unix timestamps. This is a data pipeline integration issue."

  3. Add a better fix suggestion:

    "Also validate in schema at source — prevent mixed types upstream before they enter the pipeline."

  4. Submit

    Your enhanced fix is saved to the Fix Library. Future analyses of date-column issues will show your team's recommendation first.

Issue #3 — Wave Height Outliers (MEDIUM)

ℹ️
What the AI found: 20% of Wave Height values are statistical outliers. The API's generic model flagged these as suspicious — but is it right for your domain?

Trainer guidance — reject this one (domain knowledge):

  1. Click "✗ False Positive"
  2. Add context:

    "For our coastal region, 20ft+ waves are rare but real during winter swells. This is valid data, not an error. The machine does not have seasonal domain knowledge."

  3. Submit the rejection

    The machine confidence drops from 85% → ~15%. The system has now learned a domain exception: do not flag large Wave Height values as outliers in this dataset.

🧠
What just happened: You taught the system something no generic AI knows — that 20ft waves are normal in your domain. This domain exception is now stored and will be referenced in every future analysis of similar data. This is the Wisdom Formula at work.

Step 3 — View the Fix Library

Click the Fix Library tab. You will see three new entries created from your approvals:

  • fix_SURF_001 — Missing Surf Date Values (confidence: 95%)
  • fix_SURF_002 — Mixed Data Types in Date Column (confidence: 92%)
  • fix_SURF_003 — Wave Height Domain Exception (confidence: 98%)

Step 4 — Generate a Report

Click Report → Generate Quality Report. Download the plain-text report and share it with your team. The report includes confidence scores, who approved each finding, and the reasoning your team captured.

Expected outcome: You have established a quality baseline (84.0), captured three domain-specific knowledge items, and your Fix Library is seeded for future analyses.

Scenario 2 · Data Quality Degradation

🗑️ Slop Data — When Good Data Goes Bad

Experience what happens when data quality gradually deteriorates. This scenario uses e-commerce pricing data that degrades from perfect to dangerously sloppy over a period of months.

"Slop isn't noise — it's a slow leak. By the time you notice, the pipeline is already wrong."

What Is Slop Data?

Slop data is data that appears valid on the surface but contains subtle quality failures — inconsistent formats, partially missing values, mixed types, or values just barely outside acceptable ranges. Unlike catastrophic failures, slop accumulates silently and is the primary cause of model drift and AI hallucinations.

⚠️
Why slop is dangerous: At 95% quality your model still produces reasonable outputs. At 85% it starts making wrong recommendations. At 68% it is actively misleading — but it keeps running, no errors, no alerts. Slop fails silently.

Scenario 3 · Model Drift

📉 Drift Detection — When Your Model Stops Knowing What It Knows

Use the Drift Monitor to detect when data quality changes are causing your AI model's accuracy to silently degrade month over month.

"The model was 94% accurate in January. It's 71% accurate today. Nobody noticed. That's drift — and it's the most expensive bug that never throws an error."

What Is Model Drift?

Drift occurs when the statistical properties of incoming data change from what the model was trained on. The model's code is unchanged, tests still pass, but its predictions become progressively wrong. Drift is the number one cause of AI model failures in production.

GuardRails detects drift by tracking human approval accuracy month over month. When the proportion of AI-detected issues that humans validate as real starts falling, it means the AI's detection is becoming less aligned with your domain — that's drift.

Prerequisites for This Scenario

ℹ️
Run Scenario 2 (Slop Data) first to generate approval history across all six months. The Drift Monitor reads from that approval history to calculate accuracy trends.

How to Run the Drift Detection Scenario

Step 1 — Run all six months from Scenario 2

Upload and approve issues for all six drift files in sequence. Make sure to approve issues that are genuine and reject any that are domain-specific false positives.

Step 2 — Open the Drift Monitor tab

  1. Click "⚠️ Drift Monitor" in the navigation menu

    The tab loads your monthly accuracy trend automatically.

  2. Read the accuracy bar chart

    Each bar represents one calendar month. The height shows the percentage of AI-flagged issues your team confirmed as real. A healthy model stays above 80%.

  3. Look for the red drift alert

    If accuracy drops more than 10 percentage points in a single month, a red alert appears: "Drift detected — accuracy dropped X%. Consider retraining."

Step 3 — Interpret the Drift Signal

Accuracy TrendWhat It MeansAction Required
Stable above 85%Model is well-aligned with your dataContinue monitoring monthly
Gradual decline 85% → 75%Data is slowly shifting from training distributionQueue retraining within 30 days
Sharp drop >10% in one monthSomething structural changed — supplier, format, domainImmediate investigation required
Recovery after dropData team intervention workedExport new training data to capture the improved baseline

Step 4 — Export Training Data for Retraining

  1. Click "Export Training Data (JSON)"

    Downloads a labeled dataset of every issue your team has reviewed, with human verdicts attached. This is ground truth for retraining a custom model.

  2. Or click "Export as CSV"

    Same data in spreadsheet format — usable by data scientists in pandas, R, or Excel.

  3. Share with your ML team

    The export includes: analysis IDs, timestamps, API scores, detected issues, and human approval status. Everything needed to retrain the model on your domain data.

🧠
Why this matters: Most teams detect model drift months after it starts — only when business metrics decline. GuardRails detects it in the data itself, before it reaches your model. Catching drift at month 3 (93%) costs hours to fix. Catching it at month 5 (68%) costs weeks.

Scenario 4 · AI Readiness

🤖 Hallucination Risk — Is Your Data Safe to Feed an AI?

Discover how data quality issues translate directly into AI model failures: hallucinations, biased predictions, and incorrect recommendations — all caused by the data, not the model.

"Your AI model is only as good as the data it was trained on. Garbage in, confident hallucinations out."

What Is an AI Hallucination?

An AI hallucination is when a model produces output that is confidently stated but factually wrong or nonsensical. In data-driven models, hallucinations are almost always caused by data quality failures — the model learned from bad data and generalized that badness into its predictions.

Common hallucination triggers in data:

Data IssueAI Model BehaviorReal-World Impact
Null rate above 5%Model fills gaps with statistically likely but wrong valuesWrong pricing, incorrect patient risk scores
Mixed field formatsModel treats different formats as different categoriesFraud model misses patterns it learned in a different format
Duplicate recordsModel over-weights duplicated examples, creates biasProduct recommendations skewed toward duplicated items
Non-numeric values in numeric fieldsSilent coercion to NaN or zero — model sees phantom zerosHealthcare model predicts zero medication dosage
Unparseable datesModel loses temporal context — seasonality disappearsForecasting model treats all months as equal

Running the Hallucination Risk Scenario

Phase A — Upload a File Likely to Cause Hallucinations

  1. Upload Month 5 data (68% quality)

    Drag 📄 Data/ecom_drift_month5_june_68pct.csv onto the upload zone and analyze it.

  2. Note the score breakdown

    Observe which specific dimensions are failing. Low Completeness (high null rate) and low Consistency (mixed types) are the two highest hallucination risk factors.

Phase B — Review Issues as an AI Risk Assessor

When reviewing issues, shift your mindset: instead of "Is this a data problem?" ask "Would this cause an AI model to learn the wrong thing?"

  1. For every missing value issue — approve and tag it as "AI risk"

    Add context: "This null value would be imputed as the column mean — creating phantom data the model treats as real."

  2. For every format inconsistency — approve and explain the hallucination mechanism

    Add context: "Mixed price formats ($45 vs 45.00 vs 45,00) will be parsed differently by different pipeline stages — the model will see three separate data distributions where there should be one."

  3. For duplicate records — approve with bias impact context

    Add context: "Duplicated product records will cause the model to over-fit to those specific products. Recommendations will be systematically biased toward duplicated SKUs."

Phase C — Understand the Hallucination Verdicts (Coming Soon: AI Readiness Score)

The upcoming AI Readiness Score feature will automatically assess whether your data is safe for AI pipeline use. Until it ships, use these manual thresholds:

🟢
AI Ready — Score 90+ with no null rate issues Your data meets the minimum quality threshold for AI pipeline use. Completeness is above 95%, types are consistent, no duplicate keys, numeric and date fields are well-formed.
🟡
Needs Work — Score 75–89 or null rate 5–15% Specific issues would cause AI model drift or incorrect predictions. The model can still run, but its outputs require human validation before acting on them.
🔴
Not AI Ready — Score below 75 or null rate above 15% This data would actively harm an AI model's accuracy. Training or running inference on this data will produce hallucinations. Do not feed this to a model without remediation.

Chaos Engineering · Powered by Chaos Toolkit

🔥 Chaos Data Mode

Deliberately inject data corruption into your pipeline to discover how — and exactly where — quality failures break downstream systems and AI models.

"Don't wait for production to find your fragile fields. Break them on purpose — in a controlled environment — and fix them before they cost you."

What Is Chaos Engineering for Data?

Chaos engineering was pioneered by Netflix to test infrastructure resilience by deliberately introducing failures. GuardRails applies the same discipline to data quality: we inject controlled corruption into your data, score the result, and reveal which fields and pipelines are most fragile under real-world stress conditions.

ℹ️
Chaos Toolkit is the open-source Python framework powering GuardRails Chaos Mode. Experiments are defined as JSON or YAML, executed via the CLI, and can call any HTTP endpoint — including the GuardRails /chaos-test API.

Install Chaos Toolkit

Chaos Toolkit requires Python 3.8+. Install into your virtual environment:

# Create and activate a virtual environment (if you haven't)
python -m venv .venv

# Windows
.venv\Scripts\activate

# Mac/Linux
source .venv/bin/activate

# Install Chaos Toolkit core + HTTP driver
pip install chaostoolkit
pip install chaostoolkit-lib

# Verify installation
chaos --version
💡
The project .venv is already configured. If you have already run Activate.ps1, just run pip install chaostoolkit chaostoolkit-lib.

How GuardRails Chaos Mode Works

Every Chaos Toolkit experiment has three parts:

PartPurposeIn GuardRails
Steady-State HypothesisDefine what "normal" looks like before chaosBaseline data quality score (e.g., ≥ 80)
Method (Chaos Actions)Inject the failureNull 10% of fields, scramble formats, duplicate 5% of rows
RollbackRestore system to safe stateOriginal file preserved; corrupted copy discarded

The Three Chaos Injections

Injection TypeWhat HappensFields Targeted
NULL STORMRandomly sets 10% of all field values to null/emptyAll columns — reveals which are load-bearing
FORMAT CHAOSScrambles expected formats in key columnsDate, email, phone, ID fields specifically
DUPLICATE FLOODRandomly clones 5% of records and re-inserts themEntire rows — tests deduplication resilience

Running Chaos Mode — In-App

  1. Upload your clean baseline file

    Use any file that already scored well (80+). The chaos test requires a known-good baseline to compare against.

  2. Click "Run Chaos Test"

    Available as a button on the Upload tab after a successful analysis. The server makes a copy of your file — the original is never modified.

  3. View Before vs After score rings

    Both sets of score rings render side by side. The delta between them is your Fragility Score — higher delta = more fragile pipeline.

  4. Inspect the Fragile Fields list

    Ranked by impact. Fields that caused the largest score drop are your highest-priority hardening targets.

  5. Read the Gemini Fragility Report

    Gemini AI interprets why each fragile field failed and what downstream system — pricing model, fraud classifier, readmission predictor — would break first.

Running Chaos Mode — Chaos Toolkit CLI

For CI/CD integration, automated pipelines, or scheduled chaos runs, use the Chaos Toolkit CLI with a GuardRails experiment file.

Step 1 — Create the experiment file

Save this as chaos-experiments/data-quality-baseline.json:

{
  "version": "1.0.0",
  "title": "GuardRails Data Quality Chaos Experiment",
  "description": "Verify data pipeline resilience by injecting controlled corruption and measuring quality score degradation.",
  "tags": ["data-quality", "guardrails", "syswisdom"],

  "steady-state-hypothesis": {
    "title": "Data quality score is at or above acceptable threshold",
    "probes": [
      {
        "type": "probe",
        "name": "baseline-quality-score",
        "tolerance": {
          "type": "jsonpath",
          "path": "$.overall_score",
          "expect": {
            "gte": 80
          }
        },
        "provider": {
          "type": "http",
          "url": "http://localhost:3000/history",
          "method": "GET",
          "timeout": 10
        }
      }
    ]
  },

  "method": [
    {
      "type": "action",
      "name": "inject-null-storm",
      "description": "Randomly null 10% of fields in the target dataset",
      "provider": {
        "type": "http",
        "url": "http://localhost:3000/chaos-test",
        "method": "POST",
        "headers": { "Content-Type": "application/json" },
        "arguments": {
          "chaos_type": "null_storm",
          "null_pct": 0.10,
          "analysis_id": "${analysis_id}"
        },
        "timeout": 30
      }
    },
    {
      "type": "action",
      "name": "inject-format-chaos",
      "description": "Scramble date, email, and ID field formats",
      "provider": {
        "type": "http",
        "url": "http://localhost:3000/chaos-test",
        "method": "POST",
        "headers": { "Content-Type": "application/json" },
        "arguments": {
          "chaos_type": "format_chaos",
          "target_columns": ["date", "email", "id", "phone"],
          "analysis_id": "${analysis_id}"
        },
        "timeout": 30
      }
    },
    {
      "type": "action",
      "name": "inject-duplicate-flood",
      "description": "Clone and re-insert 5% of records to test deduplication",
      "provider": {
        "type": "http",
        "url": "http://localhost:3000/chaos-test",
        "method": "POST",
        "headers": { "Content-Type": "application/json" },
        "arguments": {
          "chaos_type": "duplicate_flood",
          "duplicate_pct": 0.05,
          "analysis_id": "${analysis_id}"
        },
        "timeout": 30
      }
    }
  ],

  "rollbacks": [
    {
      "type": "action",
      "name": "restore-original-data",
      "description": "Discard corrupted copy — original file is preserved server-side",
      "provider": {
        "type": "http",
        "url": "http://localhost:3000/chaos-rollback",
        "method": "POST",
        "arguments": { "analysis_id": "${analysis_id}" }
      }
    }
  ]
}

Step 2 — Run the experiment

# Run the chaos experiment
chaos run chaos-experiments/data-quality-baseline.json

# Run with verbose output (recommended for first run)
chaos --verbose run chaos-experiments/data-quality-baseline.json

# Generate a full HTML report
pip install chaostoolkit-reporting
chaos report --export-format=html journal.json report.html

Using YAML Instead of JSON

Prefer YAML? Save as chaos-experiments/data-quality-baseline.yaml:

version: "1.0.0"
title: GuardRails Data Quality Chaos Experiment
description: >
  Verify data pipeline resilience by injecting controlled corruption
  and measuring quality score degradation.
tags:
  - data-quality
  - guardrails
  - syswisdom

steady-state-hypothesis:
  title: Data quality score is at or above acceptable threshold
  probes:
    - type: probe
      name: baseline-quality-score
      tolerance:
        type: jsonpath
        path: "$.overall_score"
        expect:
          gte: 80
      provider:
        type: http
        url: http://localhost:3000/history
        method: GET
        timeout: 10

method:
  - type: action
    name: inject-null-storm
    description: Randomly null 10% of fields in the target dataset
    provider:
      type: http
      url: http://localhost:3000/chaos-test
      method: POST
      arguments:
        chaos_type: null_storm
        null_pct: 0.10

rollbacks:
  - type: action
    name: restore-original-data
    provider:
      type: http
      url: http://localhost:3000/chaos-rollback
      method: POST

Run chaos experiments automatically on every pull request or nightly. Example GitHub Actions step:

🧠 The Wisdom Formula

∞
(Experience / Wisdom) ^ Time
The core equation behind everything GuardRails builds.

In GuardRails, the Wisdom Formula is implemented as:

OVERALL SCORE = Consistency × Validity × Completeness ^ Team_Experience

Where:
  Consistency    = All anomalies detected (AI detection rate)
  Validity       = Issues confirmed real by humans (approval rate)
  Completeness   = Rules coverage across all columns
  Team_Experience = Number of files your team has analyzed

The formula explains why early scores feel conservative and later scores feel authoritative — both are correct. Early on, wisdom is still building. Over time, each validation compounds, and the system becomes exponentially better at predicting what matters to your specific team in your specific domain.
